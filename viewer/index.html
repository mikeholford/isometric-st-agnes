St Agnes

The mining village on the north coast of Cornwall in miniature, from Chapel Porth and Wheal Coates over the Beacon to Trevaunance Cove.

Every house, every hedge and every tree stands in its real place, and the land has its real shape, rebuilt from open maps and laser height measurements. Scroll or pinch to zoom, or look up a street or place below. Point at a road to see its name. Hold ⌘ (or Ctrl) and drag to turn the map.

Bolster the giant is out walking, on a road, a path or the sand. Everyone sees him in the same place. Find him and click him.

Make this for your own village

This map follows Isometric Helenaveen by @jankeesvw, who rebuilt his Dutch village in isometric pixel art with Claude Code and nothing but open data, and shared the code and this prompt.

Want one of your own village or town, anywhere in the world? Paste this prompt into Claude Code or another coding agent and fill in your place. The agent finds the open data for your country: buildings, heights, aerial photos and OpenStreetMap. Then tell it the stories that only the people who live there know.

Make an isometric pixel-art map of [your village or town, and the country], like Isometric Helenaveen: https://jankeesvw.github.io/isometric-helenaveen/

Use the code at https://github.com/jankeesvw/isometric-helenaveen as an example. Clone the repo and read the README first: it describes the whole pipeline, from open data to the viewer.

The example uses Dutch open data: RD coordinates (EPSG:28992), buildings from the 3D BAG, the ground from the BGT, an aerial photo from PDOK and the heights of trees and terrain from the AHN. Find the open data for my country that comes closest and adapt the scripts to it:
- a metric coordinate system for the area, for example the right UTM zone;
- buildings with heights and ideally roof shapes: a national 3D building dataset, or else the buildings from OpenStreetMap or Overture Maps with heights from an elevation model;
- a surface model and a bare-earth elevation model, for example national lidar data or a global model, for the trees and the terrain;
- an aerial photo or a land-use map to colour the ground;
- OpenStreetMap for roads, water, paths and the traffic, which works everywhere.
If my place is in the Netherlands, you can use the example's sources as they are.

Then:
1. Set the area, a few kilometres square, in scripts/config.py.
2. Run ./build.sh, look at the result with python3 -m http.server 8000 -d site and show me screenshots.
3. Replace the places in scripts/landmarks.py with buildings and places from my village or town, each with a short story. Take out or replace what belongs to Helenaveen only: the tour (scripts/build_history.py) and the fixed spots in viewer/index.html, such as the starting point, the village centre for the street lamps and the swimmers in the canal.
4. Put the map online, for example with GitHub Pages (deploy.sh).

Work step by step, check the licences of the data you use, and ask me about the stories and places that only the people who live there know.
